The blue lines are where the station takes the board's wires to be. They should sit on the wires, with each number by its own segment. If they don't, press "Calibrate again" with nothing in front of the board.

Playing DartCounter through OBS? Press "Let OBS have the cameras", then start OBS. Leave this page open: it takes the cameras back by itself a few seconds after OBS closes. (OBS already open, with black cameras? Start OBS again: the station let go as soon as it saw OBS.)

This window is the camera station: leave it open, and don't use it for anything else (it can be behind other windows; the links at the top open in a new tab). Closing it, or going to another page in it, stops the cameras. The computer must not go to sleep while you play. Only this page reads the cameras: your games, on any computer or tablet signed in to the same account, get the darts from it.

Set up the cameras

Point the cameras at the board so each sees all of it, take the darts out, and press the button. The station finds the cameras that can see the board, calibrates each one to the board by itself, and starts. Nothing to choose and nothing to tap.

    Choose the cameras yourself, and other settings

    Using OBS? While OBS is open it holds the cameras: choose its virtual camera ("OBS Virtual Camera", or "OBS") for both. Each half of its picture is calibrated on its own.

    A camera turned on its side or upside down can't tell which way up the board is by itself: calibrate that one by hand, once, on the Cameras page. After that it keeps itself calibrated like the others.

    Learning from your games

    Every dart the cameras put into a game is kept here with its pictures once its visit is over: a dart you left in was read right, and one you corrected (Undo and tap, or Edit my last visit) shows where it really landed. Every few corrections the station reads them all again with other settings, and uses settings that read clearly more of them right.
